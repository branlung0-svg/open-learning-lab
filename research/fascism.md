# Fascism and Nazism deep dive: research brief (v0.1, 14 Nov 2026)

Status: **research in progress**. Every figure below has its source next to it. Nothing here ships to the site until it becomes a claim record in `data/claims/fascism.json` and passes the independent check.

The deep dive uses the same tabs and the same standard of evidence as the Marxism–Leninism and capitalism dives, so the three can be compared side by side. Two rules matter most here and override everything else on these pages:

1. **Settled facts are stated plainly.** The Holocaust happened. About six million Jews were murdered. The evidence comes from German records, the killers' own testimony, survivors, liberators, demographic data, physical sites and postwar trials. Denial and minimization are never presented as a "side". They can be taught only as a subject of study (who spreads them, why, and how historians answer them), on a separate, clearly labelled page.
2. **Debates are about interpretation.** Historians argue about definitions, causes, timing, motives and comparisons. They do not argue about whether the crimes happened.

Sensitivity notes for the build: images of the camps, shootings and the dead are blurred until tapped and hidden for ages 10–12. Perpetrator propaganda is shown only as an analysed object (who made it, for whom, what it hid), never as a standalone image or clip, and never quoted at length. No perpetrator execution or atrocity footage made to spread terror. Victims are named where known.

## 2. Sourced numbers (first batch)

**The Holocaust: about six million Jews.** USHMM's best estimate is 6 million Jews murdered. Its breakdown by place: Auschwitz complex about 1 million; Treblinka about 925,000; Belzec 434,508; Sobibor at least 167,000; Chelmno 156,000–172,000; shootings and gas vans in the occupied Soviet Union at least 1.3 million; ghettos at least 800,000; shootings in the General Government at least 200,000; Croatia under the Ustaše 23,000–25,000. Source: [USHMM, Documenting Numbers of Victims of the Holocaust and Nazi Persecution](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/documenting-numbers-of-victims-of-the-holocaust-and-nazi-persecution) (figures read via the [Jewish Virtual Library reproduction, which credits USHMM](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/documenting-numbers-of-victims-of-the-holocaust); the USHMM page could not be opened directly from this session; re-check). Yad Vashem: approximately six million; no precise figure exists; its country-by-country table gives about 5.6 to 5.86 million; earlier scholarly estimates range from 5.1 million (Hilberg) to about 5.95 million; Wolfgang Benz's team gave 5.3–6.2 million. Its Central Database holds somewhat more than 4.7 million victims' names, mostly from about 2.5 million Pages of Testimony. Source: [Yad Vashem FAQs](https://yadvashem.org/holocaust/faqs); [Yad Vashem names database](https://www.yadvashem.org/collections). How it is known: prewar and postwar censuses, German deportation and shooting reports (for example Einsatzgruppen reports), camp records, survivor and perpetrator testimony, trial evidence. Confidence: **Established** (the range 5.1–6 million is a debate about counting method, not about whether it happened).

**Other victims of Nazi persecution (USHMM best estimates).** Soviet civilians about 7 million (including 1.3 million Soviet Jews, also counted in the 6 million); Soviet POWs about 3 million; non-Jewish Polish civilians about 1.8 million; Serb civilians in the Ustaše state 312,000; disabled people in institutions up to 250,000; Roma up to 250,000; Jehovah's Witnesses about 1,900; "repeat offenders" and "asocials" at least 70,000; homosexual men hundreds, possibly thousands. Source: as above. Confidence: **Strong** (ranges differ by source and definition).

**Roma and Sinti.** Germany and its allies killed at least 250,000 European Roma; some scholars put the toll near 500,000; the exact number is unknown. Prewar European Romani population: 1–1.5 million. Of about 23,000 Roma and Sinti deported to Auschwitz-Birkenau, about 21,000 died; nearly all of Croatia's roughly 25,000 Roma were killed; at least 30,000 shot by German units in the occupied Soviet Union; about 26,000 deported by Romania to Transnistria. Source: [USHMM, Genocide of European Roma](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/genocide-of-european-roma-gypsies-1939-1945). Confidence: **Strong** (fact of genocide); **Contested** (total, 200,000–500,000). Note: USHMM's numbers page says "up to 250,000" and its Roma article says "at least 250,000". Flag for the fact-checker.

**Aktion T4 and the murder of disabled people.** T4's own internal count: 70,273 people killed in six gassing centers (Brandenburg, Grafeneck, Bernburg, Sonnenstein, Hartheim, Hadamar) from January 1940 to August 1941. All phases together: about 250,000. At least 10,000 disabled children. Hitler's authorization was signed in autumn 1939 and backdated to 1 September 1939. The T4 staff and gas technology were later used in the death camps. Source: [USHMM, Nazi "Euthanasia" Program](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/euthanasia-program). Confidence: **Established**.

**Soviet prisoners of war.** About 5.7 million captured; about 3.3 million (57%) dead by the end of the war. For comparison, about 3.6% of 231,000 British and American POWs died in German custody. USHMM states the toll was deliberate policy. Source: [USHMM, Nazi Persecution of Soviet Prisoners of War](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/nazi-persecution-of-soviet-prisoners-of-war). Confidence: **Established**.

**Generalplan Ost.** Plan devised 1941–42 to resettle Eastern Europe with Germans: about 31 million people, mostly Slavs, to be expelled to western Siberia; the rest enslaved, "Germanized" or killed; Jews to be annihilated; about 10 million Germans to be settled. Partial implementation: expulsions of Poles from the Zamość region, November 1942–August 1943. Source: [Yad Vashem Shoah Resource Center entry, Generalplan Ost (PDF)](https://wwv.yadvashem.org/odot_pdf/Microsoft%20Word%20-%206247.pdf). Confidence: **Strong** (the plan survives in drafts; figures vary between versions). To fetch: the 1942 Meyer draft and the "Hunger Plan" (Backe), with Gerlach and Snyder's estimates.

**Nazi election results 1928–1933 (Reichstag).** 20 May 1928: 2.6%. 14 September 1930: 18.3% (second-largest party). 31 July 1932: 37.3% (largest party; their peak in a free election). 6 November 1932: 33.1%. 5 March 1933: 43.9%, under police terror after the Reichstag fire decree; with the DNVP the coalition passed 50%. Hitler was **appointed** chancellor by President Hindenburg on 30 January 1933, at the head of a coalition, after a deal brokered by Franz von Papen. Source: [USHMM, The Nazi Rise to Power](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-nazi-rise-to-power) (gives 2.6, 18, 37, 33 and "almost 44" percent; the decimals are the standard official figures and should be confirmed against the Statistisches Reichsamt or Falter's dataset). Confidence: **Established**.

**Who voted Nazi (studies).** King, Rosen, Tanner and Wagner (*Journal of Economic History* 68:4, 2008) find that voters hit hardest by the Depression split into groups with different interests: some moved to the Nazis and others away; they argue the voting behavior was ordinary even if the result was not. Note: the commissioning brief named "King, Tomz and Wittenberg"; the 2008 paper's authors are King, Rosen, Tanner and Wagner. Source: [Gary King's page](https://gking.harvard.edu/publication/ordinary-economic-voting-behavior-in-the-extraordinary-election-of-adolf-hitler/); [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jechis:v:68:y:2008:i:04:p:951-996_00). The group-level findings (working poor and self-employed toward the Nazis; unemployed toward the Communists) are in the paper body: confirm before use. Other evidence: districts more exposed to the 1931 Danatbank failure swung more to the Nazis, especially where antisemitism had deep roots ([Doerr, Gissler, Peydró and Voth, *Journal of Finance* 77:6, 2022](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:77:y:2022:i:6:p:3339-3372)); districts with more austerity (spending cuts, tax rises) had higher Nazi vote shares ([Galofré-Vilà, Meissner, McKee and Stuckler, *JEH* 81:1, 2021](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jechis:v:81:y:2021:i:1:p:81-113_3)); towns with denser club networks saw faster Nazi party entry ([Satyanath, Voigtländer and Voth, *JPE* 125:2, 2017](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ucp:jpolec:doi:10.1086/690949)); places with medieval pogroms showed more antisemitic violence in the 1920s–30s ([Voigtländer and Voth, *QJE* 127:3, 2012](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:127:y:2012:i:3:p:1339-1392)). Confidence: **Strong** for each finding; how they add up is **Contested** (debate 3E).

**Italian colonial violence: Libya.** During the "pacification" of Cyrenaica (about 1929–1934), 100,000–110,000 people, mostly women, children and the elderly, were forced into desert concentration camps; estimates of deaths in deportation and the camps run from about 40,000 to 70,000 (Emilio Gentile gives about 50,000 deaths from the suppression of resistance). Source: [Wikipedia, Italian concentration camps in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_concentration_camps_in_Libya) (secondary; replace with Ali Abdullatif Ahmida, *Genocide in Libya*, 2020, and Giorgio Rochat). Confidence: **Strong** (camps and mass death); **Contested** (the toll, and whether to call it genocide).

**Ethiopia, including poison gas.** Italy invaded on 3 October 1935 and took Addis Ababa on 5 May 1936; League of Nations sanctions failed ([Britannica, Italo-Ethiopian War](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/297461)). Italian forces used chemical weapons, chiefly mustard gas sprayed and dropped from aircraft, including on Red Cross facilities, in breach of the 1925 Geneva Protocol, which Italy had ratified ([International Review of the Red Cross article on the ICRC and chemical warfare in the Italo-Ethiopian war, via Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-review-of-the-red-cross-1961-1997/article/force-versus-law-the-international-committee-of-the-red-cross-and-chemical-warfare-in-the-italoethiopian-war-19351936/364DE165BB03641DE46708B573B0A966); [SIPRI background paper](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/Italo-Ethiopian-war.pdf)). Italy officially admitted the gas use only in 1995–96 (after Angelo Del Boca's research; source to fetch). Confidence: **Established**. Total Ethiopian deaths 1935–41: Ethiopia's postwar claim of about 760,000 is not independently verified (to fetch; mark **Unknown** until sourced).

**Addis Ababa, 19–21 February 1937 (Yekatit 12).** After a grenade attack on Viceroy Rodolfo Graziani, Italian Blackshirts, soldiers and civilians killed residents across the city for three days. Estimates: about 3,000 (Del Boca, Mockler); 1,400–6,000 (contemporary foreign press); about 10,000 (Pankhurst, per Bosworth); about 19,000–19,200 (Ian Campbell, 2017, using oral testimony and diplomatic reports); 30,000 (Ethiopian sources). At Debre Libanos monastery in May 1937, Italian records say 297 monks and 23 laymen were shot; Campbell estimates far more. Sources: [Wikipedia, Yekatit 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekatit_12); [History Today review of Campbell by R. J. B. Bosworth](https://www.historytoday.com/reviews/addis-ababa-massacre). Confidence: **Established** (the massacre); **Contested** (toll).

**Spanish Civil War and Francoist repression.** About 500,000 deaths in the war overall ([Britannica summary](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Spanish-Civil-War)). Killings behind the lines: Republican-zone killings about 49,000–55,000; Nationalist and Francoist killings, during the war and after it, about 130,000–150,000 or more. Named estimates: Preston (*The Spanish Holocaust*, 2012) about 150,000 Francoist (roughly three times the Republican 50,000) ([Maclean's review](https://cms.macleans.ca/culture/books/review-the-spanish-holocaust-inquisition-and-extermination-in-twentieth-century-spain/)); Espinosa Maestre (2010) 130,199 Francoist (including postwar) and 49,272 Republican; Casanova 100,000 and 55,000 (wartime only); Juliá et al. (1999) about 72,500 documented in half of Spain's provinces; Payne (2012) somewhat over 120,000 executions by both sides during the war. The old Francoist-era count (Salas Larrazábal, 1977: 35,500 Nationalist, 72,500 Republican) has been overtaken by province-by-province research. Source for the comparison: [summary of the historiography (Wikipedia mirror)](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Historiography_on_Spanish_Civil_War_repressions_(numbers)) (replace with the original works). Confidence: **Strong** (the asymmetry and order of magnitude); **Contested** (exact totals, and what to count). Mass graves: the Spanish government's figure of about 114,000 disappeared is widely cited; source to fetch.

**Japanese atrocities: Nanjing, December 1937–early 1938.** Britannica: most estimates of Chinese killed run from 100,000 to more than 300,000 ([Britannica, Nanjing Massacre](https://www.britannica.com/event/Nanjing-Massacre)). Named estimates: the Tokyo tribunal tallied about 155,000 from burial records and its verdict against General Matsui said upwards of 100,000; the postwar Nanjing War Crimes Tribunal gave over 300,000 (the figure used by the People's Republic of China; critics point to errors in its burial data); Kasahara Tokushi about 160,000–170,000 (well over 100,000, possibly about 200,000); Hata Ikuhiko about 40,000 (38,000–42,000); John Rabe, who was there, 50,000–60,000; Lewis Smythe's 1938 survey found about 12,000 civilians killed in the city and 26,870 in surrounding counties. Much of the spread comes from definitions: the area (city or city plus six counties), the period (six weeks or until March 1938), and whether executed prisoners and soldiers out of uniform count. Source: [Wikipedia, Death toll of the Nanjing Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_toll_of_the_Nanjing_Massacre) (secondary; to fetch: IMTFE judgment text, which also states "over 200,000" in its general findings; Kasahara; Hata; the 2010 Japan–China joint history report). Confidence: **Established** (mass killing, rape and looting); **Contested** (toll). Note: the Japanese "illusion school" that denies the massacre is denial, not a side.

**Unit 731.** A secret Kwantung Army unit in Manchuria that carried out human experimentation and biological-weapons research from 1933 (as the Tōgō Unit) to 1945, based at Pingfang near Harbin from 1939. Specialists widely accept that about 3,000 people were killed at Pingfang; unit records through July 1943 document 850 deaths from experiments. Field attacks with plague-infected fleas hit Chinese cities (a 1994 Chinese survey counts 106 deaths at Ningbo, 1940). Members who returned to Japan were not prosecuted; US investigators obtained their data in exchange for immunity. The Soviet Union tried 12 members at Khabarovsk in December 1949. Source: [Tsuneishi Keiichi, Asia-Pacific Journal: Japan Focus, 2005](https://apjjf.org/?p=20588). Described factually only; no images of experiments. Confidence: **Strong** (the record); **Contested** (total deaths from biological warfare in China, where much higher estimates exist; to fetch: Sheldon Harris, *Factories of Death*).

**Ustaše camps: Jasenovac.** USHMM: the Ustaše murdered between 77,000 and 99,000 people at Jasenovac (1941–1945): 45,000–52,000 Serbs; 12,000–20,000 Jews; 15,000–20,000 Roma; 5,000–12,000 Croats and Muslims (political opponents). Source: [USHMM, Jasenovac](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/jasenovac). **The contested range:** postwar Yugoslav estimates and later Serbian nationalist claims ran to 500,000–700,000 or more; Croatian nationalist claims have drastically minimized the toll or denied Jasenovac was a killing site. A review of the numbers argues that both extremes are politically driven and that name-based lists are the most reliable method ([CORE record: Numerical indicators of victims of the Jasenovac camp, 1941–1945](https://core.ac.uk/works/19793918); authors and journal to confirm). Demographic studies (Žerjavić, Kočović) and the Jasenovac Memorial Site's name list (about 83,000 names, to fetch) support the USHMM order of magnitude. Confidence: **Strong** for the USHMM range; minimization that denies mass murder is denial, not a side.

**Romania.** About 220,000 Romanian Jews were killed or died from mistreatment under Romanian authority (plus at least 90,000 from Northern Transylvania deported by Hungary); 150,000–250,000 Romanian and Ukrainian Jews were murdered or died in Romanian-run Transnistria; the Iași pogrom (June 1941) killed at least 4,000. Source: [USHMM, Romania](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/romania). To fetch: the 2004 Wiesel Commission report. Confidence: **Established** (scale); **Strong** (figures).

**France.** About 77,000 Jews living in France perished, about a third of them French citizens; about 350,000 Jews lived in France in 1940. Vichy passed its own anti-Jewish statutes (October 1940 and June 1941); French police carried out the Vél d'Hiv roundup of about 13,000 Jews, including about 4,000 children (16–17 July 1942). Sources: [USHMM, France](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/france); [USHMM, Vél d'Hiv Roundup](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-velodrome-dhiver-vel-dhiv-roundup). Confidence: **Established**.

**Hungary 1944.** About 440,000 Jews deported between 15 May and 9 July 1944, most to Auschwitz-Birkenau, by the Hungarian gendarmerie under SS guidance. Source: [USHMM timeline](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/timeline-event/holocaust/1942-1945/deportation-of-hungarian-jews). Confidence: **Established**.

**WWII total deaths.** The National WWII Museum: about 15 million battle deaths and 45 million civilian deaths (about 60 million), adding that estimates vary widely and Chinese civilian deaths alone may exceed 50 million; Soviet Union about 24 million; China about 20 million; Poland about 5.6 million; Germany 6.6–8.8 million; Japan 2.6–3.1 million ([National WWII Museum, Research Starters](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/students-teachers/student-resources/research-starters/research-starters-worldwide-deaths-world-war)). Britannica gives 35–60 million ([Britannica Q&A](https://britannica.com/question/How-many-people-died-during-World-War-II)); many recent compilations give 70–85 million (to source). Confidence: **Strong** for "tens of millions, the deadliest war in history"; **Contested** for any single total.

## 3. Debates, each side at its strongest

**A. What is fascism?**
- *Paxton (behavior, not doctrine):* *The Anatomy of Fascism* (2004) and "The Five Stages of Fascism" (*Journal of Modern History*, 1998). Judge fascism by what it did, in five stages: founding movements, rooting in the party system, taking power (always with help from conservative elites), exercising power, then radicalization or entropy. Strength: explains why only Italy and Germany reached power and why elites' choices mattered. Critics: underplays ideas; risks making fascism whatever fascists happened to do.
- *Griffin (the core myth):* *The Nature of Fascism* (1991): fascism is "palingenetic ultranationalism", a myth that the nation must be reborn from decadence through a revolutionary new order. Claimed in the 1990s as a "new consensus" (with Payne and Eatwell). Strength: one test that travels across countries and decades. Critics: too ideal-typical; marginal groups pass the test while regimes that acted fascist may fail it ([summary page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palingenetic_ultranationalism); fetch Griffin's own text).
- *Payne (a checklist):* *A History of Fascism, 1914–1945* (1995): a typology of the "fascist negations" (anti-liberal, anti-communist, anti-conservative), ideology and goals, and style and organization (mass mobilization, militia, cult of youth and leader). Strength: precise comparison; separates fascists from authoritarian conservatives (Franco, Salazar, Antonescu). Critics: checklists can be argued item by item forever.
- *Eatwell:* a "holistic-national radical Third Way" (*Fascism: A History*, 1995).
- *Mosse and Emilio Gentile (culture and political religion):* fascism as a cultural revolution with its own rituals, symbols and liturgy (Mosse, *The Nationalization of the Masses*, 1975; *The Fascist Revolution*, 1999; Gentile, *The Sacralization of Politics in Fascist Italy*, 1996). Strength: explains the appeal of rallies, myths and belonging. Critics: can read regimes through their self-image.
- *Sternhell:* fascism's ideas grew from a revision of Marxism plus nationalism in France around 1900 (*Neither Right nor Left*, 1983; *The Birth of Fascist Ideology*, 1989). Strength: takes ideas seriously; shows left-to-right migrations (Sorel, Mussolini). Critics (in France especially): overstates French fascism and the left-wing origins.
- *Marxist interpretations:* the Comintern's definition, presented by Georgi Dimitrov in 1935, called fascism in power the open, terrorist dictatorship of the most reactionary, chauvinist and imperialist elements of finance capital ([Dimitrov, 2 August 1935, marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/dimitrov/works/1935/08_02.htm)). Earlier heterodox Marxists (Thalheimer, Trotsky) saw "Bonapartism": a mass movement of the threatened middle classes that capital tolerated to crush the workers' movement. Nicos Poulantzas (*Fascism and Dictatorship*, 1970) treated it as an exceptional capitalist state arising from a crisis of political hegemony. Strength: explains business and elite cooperation, the destruction of unions, the preservation of private property. Critics: Henry Ashby Turner (*German Big Business and the Rise of Hitler*, 1985) found big business gave the Nazis relatively little before 1933; the theory struggles to explain policies that hurt capital's interests (the Holocaust's economic irrationality, war to destruction); in 1928–34 the Comintern's "social fascism" line, which treated Social Democrats as the main enemy, divided the left against Hitler.
- *Totalitarianism school:* Arendt (*The Origins of Totalitarianism*, 1951) and Friedrich and Brzezinski (1956): Nazism and Stalinism as two forms of a new kind of regime. Critics: Kershaw and Lewin (*Stalinism and Nazism*, 1997) stress structural differences.
- *Nolte and the Historikerstreit:* Ernst Nolte (*Three Faces of Fascism*, 1963) gave a phenomenological account of fascism as an anti-Marxist movement. In 1986 his article "The Past That Will Not Pass" argued that Bolshevik class murder and the Gulag were prior to and a cause of Nazi crimes, which he presented partly as a frightened reaction. Jürgen Habermas answered in *Die Zeit* (July 1986), accusing Nolte, Hillgruber and Stürmer of "apologetic tendencies" that relativized Auschwitz ([Habermas text, German History Intersections](https://germanhistory-intersections.org/en/knowledge-and-education/ghis:document-151)); Mommsen, Wehler, Jäckel and Broszat joined him; Fest and Hildebrand defended aspects of Nolte's position. Outcome: most historians rejected the "causal nexus" and any relativizing of Auschwitz, while comparative study of Nazi and Soviet crimes became normal scholarship (see Snyder, *Bloodlands*, 2010). Later critics also faulted Nolte for treating denialist claims as open questions in the 1990s (source to fetch). For German readers: [bpb, Aus Politik und Zeitgeschichte, on the Historikerstreit](https://bpb.de/shop/zeitschriften/apuz/archiv/534608/zur-kritik-des-historikerstreits).
- *Eco (popular, not scholarly):* Umberto Eco's essay "Ur-Fascism" (*New York Review of Books*, 22 June 1995) lists 14 features of "eternal fascism" and says a movement need not have all of them. Widely shared; scholars note it was a personal reflection, not a definition, and that its looseness lets the word fit almost anything. Teach it as a cultural document and test case, not as the definition. (Copyrighted: link NYRB, do not copy.)

**B. Left or right?**
- *"National Socialism was socialist" (the case at its strongest):* the party's name and 25-point program (1920) demanded nationalizing trusts, profit-sharing in big firms, abolishing "unearned income" and breaking "interest slavery" ([program text, GHDI](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/weimar-germany-1918-1933/ghdi:document-4625)). In power the state controlled prices (1936 price freeze), wages, foreign exchange, investment and raw materials (Four Year Plan, 1936); capped dividends; directed production; built a welfare state funded partly by plunder (Götz Aly, *Hitler's Beneficiaries*, 2005, though Aly himself does not argue the Nazis were "left"). Mussolini was a leading socialist editor until 1914. Mises and Hayek (*The Road to Serfdom*, 1944) argued that fascism and communism shared collectivism and central planning; the Mises Institute continues the argument ([Mises Wire, "Yes, Nazis were socialists"](https://mises.org/mises-wire/yes-nazis-were-socialists)); Jonah Goldberg's *Liberal Fascism* (2008) made a popular version.
- *"Fascism was of the right" (the mainstream historical view):* Paxton, Payne, Griffin, Evans and Kershaw note that fascists took power in alliance with conservatives, the army and business; destroyed independent unions, socialist and communist parties first; kept private ownership; rejected class struggle and equality for a hierarchical, racial nation. The SA wing that wanted a "second revolution" was purged in the Night of the Long Knives (June–July 1934). Economic historians find private property kept real meaning: firms could refuse state contracts and negotiate terms (Buchheim and Scherner, *JEH* 66:2, 2006, [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jechis:v:66:y:2006:i:02:p:390-416_00)), and the regime privatized state-owned firms in the mid-1930s (Germà Bel, *Economic History Review* 63:1, 2010, [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:ehsrev:v:63:y:2010:i:1:p:34-55)). Hitler redefined "socialism" as the racial national community, not social ownership.
- *"Neither, or a third way":* Sternhell and Eatwell: fascists themselves claimed to transcend left and right; their synthesis borrowed mass mobilization from the left and nationalism and hierarchy from the right.
- *What the page does:* shows the definitions of socialism side by side (social ownership; workers' control; extensive state direction of the economy) and lets the user test each against the evidence. Verdict for the true/false card: as historians define socialism, mostly false; the name and some policies explain why the claim persists.

**C. Intentionalists vs functionalists (how the Holocaust was decided).**
- *Intentionalists:* Lucy Dawidowicz (*The War Against the Jews*, 1975), Gerald Fleming (*Hitler and the Final Solution*, 1982), Eberhard Jäckel, Andreas Hillgruber: Hitler's long-held aim of annihilation, stated from the 1920s and in his January 1939 Reichstag "prophecy", drove the process.
- *Functionalists / structuralists:* Martin Broszat (1977), Hans Mommsen ("cumulative radicalization"), Karl Schleunes (*The Twisted Road to Auschwitz*, 1970): a chaotic polycracy of rival agencies, failed "territorial solutions" (Madagascar, Lublin), and local initiatives in the occupied East escalated step by step.
- *Synthesis (now the mainstream):* Christopher Browning (*The Origins of the Final Solution*, 2004), Ian Kershaw ("Working Towards the Führer", *Contemporary European History*, 1993, [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/contemporary-european-history/article/abs/working-towards-the-fuhrer-reflections-on-the-nature-of-the-hitler-dictatorship/AA188C6DBB8DAF4682A7093D5895A6C4)), Peter Longerich, Saul Friedländer ("redemptive antisemitism"): Hitler's ideology and authority were essential, and officials competed to anticipate his wishes. Open sub-debate on timing: summer 1941 (Breitman), October 1941 (Browning), December 1941 (Christian Gerlach). The [Wannsee Protocol](https://www.ghwk.de/en/conference/protocol-and-documents) (20 January 1942) documents coordination of a process already under way.

**D. Browning vs Goldhagen: ordinary perpetrators.**
- *Browning, Ordinary Men (1992):* Reserve Police Battalion 101, middle-aged Hamburg men, shot and deported tens of thousands of Polish Jews from July 1942. Offered the chance to step aside, a minority (roughly 10–20%) refused or evaded; most complied through conformity, deference, careerism and comradeship, with antisemitism and wartime dehumanization as background. Links to Milgram's obedience research.
- *Goldhagen, Hitler's Willing Executioners (1996):* the same battalion plus labor camps and death marches show that perpetrators were willing, even eager, because German culture held a distinct "eliminationist antisemitism". Strength: restores ideology and choice; perpetrators were not robots. Reception: huge public interest (Germany awarded him its Democracy Prize, 1997); most specialists (Browning, Hilberg, Ruth Bettina Birn) criticized the monocausal national-character thesis and the use of sources ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler%27s_Willing_Executioners); fetch the reviews).
- *Since then:* Thomas Kühne (*Belonging and Genocide*, 2010) on comradeship; Harald Welzer (*Täter*, 2005); research on non-German perpetrators and collaborators (Ukrainian, Lithuanian, Latvian, Romanian, Hungarian, French police). Both sides agree perpetrators had choices: historians have found no documented case of a German executed for refusing to kill civilians (David Kitterman, 1988; to fetch).

**E. Why did millions support it?** Each cause with its evidence:
- *Economic crisis:* German unemployment above 6 million by early 1932; the 1931 banking crisis (Doerr et al. 2022) and austerity (Galofré-Vilà et al. 2021) predict local Nazi gains; King et al. (2008) on which hard-hit groups moved.
- *Versailles and national humiliation:* territorial losses, reparations and the war-guilt clause fed the program's first demands (GHDI program).
- *Fear of communism:* the KPD won about 17% in November 1932 (to source); Italy's "Red Biennium" (1919–20) factory occupations preceded the squads; landowners and industrialists funded Fascist squads in the Po valley.
- *Nationalism and the "national community":* Peter Fritzsche (*Germans into Nazis*, 1998): many joined for national renewal, not only from despair.
- *Antisemitism:* persistence from medieval pogroms (Voigtländer and Voth 2012); propaganda effects strongest where prior antisemitism was high (Adena et al. 2015).
- *Propaganda:* radio helped the Nazis only after they controlled it, and backfired where antisemitism was historically low ([Adena, Enikolopov, Petrova, Santarosa and Zhuravskaya, *QJE* 130:4, 2015](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:qjecon:v:130:y:2015:i:4:p:1885-1939)).
- *Social networks:* civic associations spread party membership (Satyanath et al. 2017).
- *Who voted:* Thomas Childers (*The Nazi Voter*, 1983): a "catch-all" party of protest, strongest among Protestant middle classes, farmers and the self-employed; Jürgen Falter (*Hitlers Wähler*, 1991): a large working-class share too, a "people's party of protest"; Richard Hamilton (*Who Voted for Hitler?*, 1982): upper- and middle-class districts. Catholics and organized workers were the most resistant.
- *Coercion and consent:* Robert Gellately (*Backing Hitler*, 2001) stresses popular consent and denunciation; Richard Evans (*The Third Reich in Power*, 2005) stresses terror from 1933. The page shows both: support was real and terror was real.
- *Elite decisions:* in both Italy (the King refused martial law in October 1922) and Germany (Papen, Hindenburg, January 1933) conservative elites handed power to fascists who had not won majorities (Paxton's stage three).

**F. Was appeasement wrong?** Schools, following [Richard J. Evans's summary](https://richardjevans.com/?p=536): *Guilty Men* (1940) and Churchill's memoirs (weak leaders could have stopped Hitler in 1936 or 1938); A. J. P. Taylor (*The Origins of the Second World War*, 1961: Hitler was an opportunist, not a planner); the realist or "constraints" school (David Dilks and others, using archives opened in the 1960s–70s: economic weakness, Dominion reluctance, public opinion and unready air defenses made appeasement rational while Britain rearmed); counter-revisionists (R. A. C. Parker, *Chamberlain and Appeasement*, 1993: Chamberlain had alternatives, overestimated German strength, managed opinion and clung to the policy too long); John Charmley (Britain should have stayed out). Same standard for every side: the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact of August 1939 and the Soviet invasion of eastern Poland (17 September 1939) belong in the same tab, as does French policy and US isolationism. Data lab: aircraft and divisions, Britain, France, Germany, Czechoslovakia, 1938 vs 1939 (to source).

**G. How far does the word stretch today?** 
- *Use it:* Jason Stanley (*How Fascism Works*, 2018) treats fascism as a set of political tactics that can appear in democracies; Robert Paxton, long hesitant, wrote in January 2021 that the 6 January attack on the US Capitol changed his mind ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/robert-paxton-trump-fascist-opinion-1560652)); Timothy Snyder applies the term to Putin's Russia (to source).
- *Be careful:* Federico Finchelstein (*From Fascism to Populism in History*, 2017, [UC Press](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/from-fascism-to-populism-in-history/paper)) argues postwar populism is a distinct, democratic-electoral descendant of fascism, not the same thing; Cas Mudde distinguishes the "populist radical right" (accepts elections) from the extreme right; Richard Evans, Samuel Moyn and Dylan Riley argue the analogy can mislead and that today's movements lack mass militias and the drive to abolish elections. A *Fascism* journal debate (2021) collected both positions ([Paul Jackson et al., Debate: Donald Trump and fascism studies](https://nectar.northampton.ac.uk/id/eprint/16311/1/Jackson_Paul_Fascism_2021_Debate_Donald_Trump_and_Fascism_Studies.pdf)).
- *The word as a weapon on every side:* the Soviet bloc called the Berlin Wall an "anti-fascist protection rampart"; Russia called its 2022 invasion "denazification"; activists on the left and right use "fascist" and "Nazi" as insults. The lab: users take a definition (Paxton's, Griffin's or Payne's) and test it against a case, with the score shown and the scholars who disagree.
- Current events are **Developing** and stay neutral: e.g. Germany's domestic intelligence agency classified the AfD as "confirmed right-wing extremist" on 2 May 2025, suspended that pending court review, and in February 2026 the Cologne Administrative Court barred the classification for now in a ruling that is not final ([beck-aktuell](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/heute-im-recht/rechtsgeschehen/afd-rechtsextrem-einstufung-verfassungsschutz-stillhaltezusage-eilverfahren-2025-05-08); [LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/13l110925-vg-koeln-stoppt-hochstufung-afd-als-gesichert-rechtsextrem)). "Extremist" in German constitutional law is not the same claim as "fascist"; the page must say so.

## 4. True or false candidates (need a source each)

- **"Hitler was elected by a majority."** False. The Nazis peaked at 37.3% in a free election (July 1932) and fell to 33.1% in November 1932. Hindenburg appointed Hitler chancellor on 30 January 1933 after a deal among conservative politicians. Even in March 1933, under terror, they won 43.9%. Source: [USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-nazi-rise-to-power).
- **"Mussolini made the trains run on time."** Mostly false: a propaganda slogan. Much of the railway repair work was done before 1922, and travellers' accounts report continued delays. Sources found so far are popular ([HowStuffWorks](https://history.howstuffworks.com/history-vs-myth/did-mussolini-really-keep-trains-running-on-time.htm)); replace with a scholarly source (e.g. Ruth Ben-Ghiat or an Italian railway history) before use.
- **"The Nazis were socialists."** Contested in public debate; as historians define socialism, mostly false. See Debate B for both cases.
- **"Ordinary Germans did not know."** Mostly false. Many knew of deportations and mass shootings in the East, through soldiers' letters, leave stories and rumor; fewer knew details of the gas chambers. Evidence: Eric Johnson and Karl-Heinz Reuband's survey and interviews (*What We Knew*, 2005; [Times Higher Education review](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/83509)); Peter Longerich (*"Davon haben wir nichts gewusst!"*, 2006); Frank Bajohr and Dieter Pohl (2006). Exact shares to fetch. Do not use the denialist "What the Germans Knew" piece that surfaced in search.
- **"The Holocaust was planned from the start."** Partly true, partly false: see Debate C. Antisemitic persecution was central from 1933; the decision for Europe-wide mass murder came in 1941.
- **"Guernica killed 1,654 people."** Contested. The Basque government recorded 1,654 deaths; Franco-era and some later estimates put it near 120–250; Xabier Irujo argues for higher figures ([The Volunteer, 2013](https://albavolunteer.org/2013/12/the-nature-and-military-rationale-of-the-bombing-of-gernika/); [ENRS](https://enrs.eu/news/bombing-of-guernica)). The Franco regime denied the bombing until 1975.
- **"Italian Fascism was mild compared with Nazism."** Half true. Italy killed far fewer people in Europe than Germany and its occupation authorities often refused to hand Jews to the Germans before September 1943 (to source), but Fascist Italy ran deadly camps in Libya, used poison gas in Ethiopia and massacred civilians in Addis Ababa (section 2), and passed racial laws in 1938.
- **"Hitler and Mussolini took power by coup."** False for both: the March on Rome and the Beer Hall Putsch were different. Mussolini was appointed by the King while the March was under way; Hitler's 1923 putsch failed and he later came to power legally through appointment, then destroyed the constitution.
- **"Japan never apologized for its wartime crimes."** Mostly false: Prime Minister Murayama's 1995 statement apologized; how adequate later statements and textbooks have been is contested (source to fetch).
- **"The Vatican, the Allies or neutral states did nothing."** Mixed; split into separate cards with evidence (Allied knowledge of the camps by 1942; Swiss and Swedish trade; the War Refugee Board from 1944). To research.
