## What "near complete" means (the finish line)

1. **Material:** every topic page links to curated evidence (photos, film, documents, maps, data) and to live archive search. Target: 50,000+ hand-reviewed items and hundreds of millions searchable. Reviewing millions of items by hand is not realistic; the honest model is a large reviewed core plus clearly labelled live search.
2. **Depth:** every one of the 24 disciplines has the deep-dive treatment: the basics, the history of how it was discovered, uses across fields, evidence, debates, and practice.
3. **Government:** power ladders for at least 20 systems, past and present, plus "how a policy really got made" case studies and follow-the-money pages per country.
4. **Oppressor and oppressed:** at least 150 cases, each with testimony, evidence and the propaganda on every side.
5. **Balance:** at least 45% of tagged sources from outside North America and Europe, and both left- and right-leaning interpretations on every contested topic. Balance applies to interpretations and policy debates, not to settled facts: the Holocaust, the Holodomor and the Atlantic slave trade happened, and denial is never a side.
6. **Trust:** every number has a claim record with sources and a confidence label; every section is fact-checked by a separate agent; at least 10 outside expert reviewers from different regions have signed off on the sensitive sections; a public corrections log.
7. **Ease of use:** one map of the whole Lab; search that finds everything (pages, people, places, evidence); the same layout on every topic page; works well on phones; interface translated into at least 10 languages.
8. **Launch basics:** own domain, content licence, an accessibility audit (WCAG AA), privacy-friendly visitor counts, a feedback channel.

## Phases

### Phase 1: organize (next 2–3 weeks)
- A "Map of the Lab": every subject, topic, era, place and evidence type on one page, reachable in two taps.
- One page template for every topic: overview → evidence → timeline → numbers → perspectives → sources → practice.
- Search covers everything, including the power ladders, oppression cases, institutions, parliaments and countries.
- Tags connect topics across disciplines (for example famine links economics, history, biology and politics).

### Phase 2: material at scale (weeks 2–8)
- Curated media: about 1,000–2,000 new reviewed items a week through the GitHub pipeline (search → contact sheets → review → keep or drop).
- More archives in live search. Owen can register free keys for DPLA (US), Trove (Australia), DigitalNZ, a full Europeana key and a full Smithsonian key; keys let us add these without the demo limits.
- Archives from Asia, Africa and Latin America that have no API: curated link lists by country, with translation.
- Historical maps (David Rumsey Map Collection, Old Maps Online) and world news in many languages (GDELT), after testing that the site can reach them.
- Live charts from Our World in Data, World Bank, V-Dem and Maddison on every country and topic page.

### Phase 3: depth (months 1–4)
- Deep dives for the remaining disciplines, starting with economics, physics, biology, medicine and psychology.
- Power ladders: UK, Russia today, India, Iran, Japan, North Korea, Vietnam, Cuba, Saudi Arabia, the EU, Brazil, South Africa; and historical ones: the Roman Republic, Qing China, the Ottoman Empire, Mughal India, Fascist Italy, Imperial Japan, apartheid South Africa.
- Policy case studies: how the Affordable Care Act passed, how China ended the one-child policy, how a Soviet Five-Year Plan was made, Brexit.
- Oppression hub to 150+ cases.

### Phase 4: trust and launch (months 3–5)
- Fact-check every section; recruit expert reviewers; claim records for every number.
- Accessibility audit, interface translation, domain, licence.
- Soft launch to 5–20 people, then teachers and students, then public.

## Decisions only Owen can make
1. **Restart the build loop?** It is the engine for Phases 2–4. Four runs a day hit usage limits; one or two runs a day is more sustainable.
2. Buy a domain.
3. Confirm the content licence (CC BY-SA 4.0 proposed).
4. Register free API keys (DPLA, Trove, DigitalNZ, Europeana, Smithsonian).
5. Help find expert reviewers, ideally historians and political scientists from different regions.

## Honest limits
- "Checked" means checked by an AI agent until human experts review it.
- Wikimedia Commons and most open archives are richest for Europe and North America; balancing that takes curated work, not just searches.
- Live search results are not reviewed; they are labelled as such and turned off for ages 10–12.
